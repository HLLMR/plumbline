# Worked example: the name check that happened after release

This project reached public release under the name **Plumbline** without a
recorded inception name search. The omission was discovered only after the
repository, release assets, documentation, and launch copy already used the
name. Public issue [#2](https://github.com/HLLMR/plumbline/issues/2) records the
defect.

## What the late search found

The evidence was sufficient to reject continued promotion under the name:

- [`plumbline-ai`](https://pypi.org/project/plumbline-ai/) was published on
  PyPI on 2026-04-18 in the AI coding-agent category and describes hooks,
  plan-state gating, and write-time enforcement.
- [`askalf/plumbline`](https://github.com/askalf/plumbline), created before
  this project's public release, is an exact-name autonomous-agent monitoring
  project.
- [`dbreunig/plumb`](https://github.com/dbreunig/plumb) is an adjacent
  Claude-Code-oriented review and decision-extraction tool.
- The exact `plumbline` identity was already occupied across GitHub, PyPI, npm,
  and `.com` RDAP. Several exact-name GitHub repositories were in the same AI
  development category.
- The official USPTO search returned live exact marks in unrelated medical and
  religious-publishing categories. No same-category live federal mark was
  identified in this bounded screen, but that did not erase the public
  software uses above.

The complete rejected-name ledger is
[`plumbline-incident.json`](name-clearance-ledgers/plumbline-incident.json).
It records the automated request metadata and digests, the reviewed web and
USPTO evidence, classifications, limitations, and Owner rejection.

## Why the process failed

The repository had gates for authority, capability scope, review, licensing,
private-data projection, and publication. It had no gate requiring anyone to
search the proposed identity before those downstream assets were created.
Multiple agents repeated a market-differentiation claim without first
performing the cheaper factual name check. The failure was procedural and
human-visible: no required record existed, so nothing could fail closed.

The correction is not a promise that search can prove a name safe. It is a
requirement that the project preserve what it searched and force a human
decision before identity work begins.

## Replacement screen

The collector screened twenty-nine candidates across an initial availability
pass and a second pass derived from AEC hold points, permit-to-work controls,
ISO document control, and security reference-monitor concepts. Candidates
with an occupied `.com`, an exact package/repository hit, or meaningful public
use were rejected early. One apparently clean candidate, `Writrail`, was
rejected when RDAP showed `writrail.com` had been registered on 2026-08-15;
ordinary search alone had missed it.

The obvious cross-industry terms were already crowded: `Holdpoint` was occupied
on GitHub, PyPI, npm, and `.com`; `Interlock` was occupied on GitHub, npm,
crates.io, and `.com`; and `Witnesspoint` and `Datumline` had occupied
identities. Coined alternatives including `Proofwrit`, `Holdwrit`, and
`Controlwrit` survived the bounded screen but were not selected.

Three finalists survived the automated package, repository, and RDAP screen,
the broader web review, and exact/spaced official USPTO searches:

| Candidate | Material evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Writwall** | No package, repository, `.com`, or federal exact hit; one unrelated 2018 fictional use is recorded | `writ` is legalistic; `wall` foregrounds enforcement over the wider record system |
| **Grantcord** | No package, repository, `.com`, meaningful web, or federal exact hit | Meaning is less immediate and may sound like another product name until explained |
| **Writcord** | No package, repository, `.com`, meaningful web, or federal exact hit | Spoken name may be confused with "record"; `cord` needs brand explanation |

The candidate ledgers are
[`writwall-candidate.json`](name-clearance-ledgers/writwall-candidate.json),
[`grantcord-candidate.json`](name-clearance-ledgers/grantcord-candidate.json),
and [`writcord-candidate.json`](name-clearance-ledgers/writcord-candidate.json).
The Owner accepted `Writwall` on 2026-08-28 and rejected `Grantcord` and
`Writcord`. Each ledger retains that exact disposition.

## Recommendation

**Writwall** is the accepted replacement. A *writ* is recorded authority;
the *wall* is the boundary that turns an issued work order into bounded agent
capability. The name states the product's strongest distinction without
claiming that every provider surface is mechanically enforced. Its public
identity and `.com` were clear in this dated screen, subject to the limits
below.

## Limits

This case study proves that the named sources were queried, evidence was
recorded, and the stated disposition followed. It does not prove legal clearance
or guarantee that every common-law, package, repository, domain,
corporate, international, or future use was found. Search indexes and registry
results change. The ledgers expire and must be rerun immediately before the
replacement is published.
