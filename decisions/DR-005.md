D.1 Date and Owner: 2026-08-21, HLLMR.

D.2 Decision: Ratify Doctrine revision 0.8, superseding 0.7, with exactly the
    text in WO-PL-023's candidate report section 3, and no other change.

D.3 Reasoning:

    D.3.1 Appendix A's charter template asserted that violations are
    "blocked and logged" without qualification. This repository's own
    charter had already corrected that to "where enforcement exists" for its
    own use, but the correction was never ratified back into the template it
    came from. The methodology should not ship a known defect in its own
    worked example.

    D.3.2 Appendix B's "B.4 Generated boundaries," added in ratified 0.7,
    collided with the pre-existing B.4 ("BOUNDARIES") identity established
    since revision 0.5, in violation of DC.3.1's stable-identifier rule and
    DC.3.3's next-available-number rule. This is corrected by restoring B.4
    to its sole original identity and relocating the generated-boundaries
    workflow to B.7, the lawful next-available top-level letter, appended
    after B.6 rather than inserted between existing clauses. This is
    verified compatible with the shipped checker's actual marker-scanning
    behavior, which does not depend on the block's position in the document.

    D.3.3 Doctrine 5.2.1 omits `governance/templates/` though every shipped
    adoption route creates it; 5.1.3 described required dispatch-preparation
    tooling as though it were optional, though Appendix B's own text already
    presupposes such a generator/checker exists to produce the
    generated-boundaries block; and no ratified text stated that an
    assembled-but-unratified skeleton (what `init.sh`/Route C alone produces)
    is not itself adoption. All three are closed together: the layout is
    completed, 5.1.3 affirmatively requires whatever deterministic tooling
    the revision's workflow needs (described generically, never naming a
    product), and 5.1.3 states plainly that a skeleton without populated
    governance records, that tooling, and a ratified adoption record with
    the adoption commit containing it, is preparatory material under 6.1.3,
    never adoption under 6.1.1-6.1.2.

    D.3.4 Doctrine 6.1.3 said bootstrap "is not itself a work order" without
    naming the pointer-activated mechanism actually used for the active-scope
    birth test, and without distinguishing that mechanism from the
    no-work-order lockout, which activates nothing at all. 2.28 names the
    instrument precisely, narrowed to only the active-scope birth test,
    without restricting who may draft or inspect one. Recorder-closeout
    mechanics are deliberately excluded from 2.28 and reclassified as an
    Owner lifecycle action (D.3.5), because the adoption-recorder action
    they perform touches the pointer, which is one of the specific
    control-plane artifacts named in 8.7.1 — not because governance records
    are control-plane artifacts generally; only the 8.7.1 list is.

    D.3.5 RFI-22 records the Owner's disposition that the adapter should
    categorically deny writes to the active-work-order file regardless of
    grant, but no ratified clause states that rule generally, for any
    control-plane artifact, on any provider. A control-plane rule must
    govern mutation authority specifically, not read-deny declarations; must
    not forbid the wall's own append-only logging of its denials; and must
    not carve out an exception for an Owner-authored grant, which would
    recreate exactly the self-widening risk RFI-22 identified under a
    different label. Part 8.7 makes the rule unconditional and reclassifies
    activation, retirement, installed-configuration changes, pointer or
    active-record changes, a recorder action that itself mutates a
    control-plane artifact, and the adoption-recorder lifecycle action, as
    Owner lifecycle actions performed outside any capability grant (8.7.6) —
    never as a grant-based exception to 8.7.2 — leaving ordinary Part 7
    closeout, history, and reporting governed as before, with clerical
    execution delegable to a separately authorized recorder, authority
    staying with the Owner, and that authorization recorded before execution
    in a durable Owner disposition or lifecycle packet rather than in chat
    alone. The birth test itself still needs a lawful way to name its own
    canary target: 8.7.4.1-8.7.4.3 supply a narrow, machine-readable
    exception in which a birth-test instrument declares `instrument_kind:
    birth-test` and lists the exact control-plane path in
    `control_plane_probes`, which confers no authority under 8.7.2, must
    match its grant target exactly with no unlabeled control-plane path left
    over, and must still be denied by the runtime wall; an ordinary
    candidate naming any control-plane path in its grant fails dispatch
    outright regardless of labeling. Part 8.7 ratifies this as methodology,
    explicitly not claiming any adapter yet implements or has birth-tested
    it (8.7.4).

D.4 Rejected alternatives:

    D.4.1 Renumber the generated-boundaries block to B.3.1, treating it as a
    simple duplicate. Rejected on review: 0.7 ratified "B.4 Generated
    boundaries" as such, so the fix is not a fresh choice of any convenient
    number but a correction of a specific DC.3.1/DC.3.3 violation, which
    requires restoring B.4's original identity and giving the new material
    its own lawful next-available number (B.7), not folding it under B.3.

    D.4.2 Leave Appendix A's overclaim as project-specific judgment.
    Rejected: the methodology's own worked example already needed the fix;
    leaving the canonical template wrong manufactures repeated,
    independent rediscovery of the same correction.

    D.4.3 Fix only the `governance/templates/` omission and leave the
    scaffold-versus-adoption distinction to distribution prose alone.
    Rejected: that distinction is load-bearing (Route C exists specifically
    to produce a skeleton, not completed adoption) and belongs in ratified
    Doctrine text so it cannot silently drift out of a future distribution's
    documentation.

    D.4.4 Define the birth-test instrument broadly enough to cover recorder
    closeout as well as the active-scope birth test, and forbid
    Dispatcher/Reviewer involvement categorically. Rejected twice, on two
    successive returns: first because most bootstrap work needs no pointer
    at all and Doctrine should not fix who may draft or inspect one; second,
    on this return, because the adoption-recorder action necessarily touches
    the pointer — one of the specific control-plane artifacts in 8.7.1, not
    "governance records" generally — and folding it into "birth-test
    instrument" invited exactly the 8.7.2/8.7.5 contradiction this decision
    now closes. 2.28 is narrowed to the active-scope birth test only, with
    only the exact, labeled falsification-probe exception of
    8.7.4.1-8.7.4.3.

    D.4.5 Extend the protected-control-plane rule to `filesystem.read.deny`
    as well as `filesystem.write`. Rejected: a read-deny entry authorizes no
    mutation and creates no self-widening risk; extending the rule there
    would conflate two unrelated concerns and could be misread as forbidding
    ordinary, harmless read restrictions on control-plane paths.

    D.4.6 State a general "bootstrap exemption" from 8.7.2 for any
    Owner-authorized instrument, including letting the Owner personally
    author a grant that gives a birth-test instrument mutation authority
    over a control-plane artifact. Rejected on this return, having been
    identified as a live contradiction with 8.7.2's categorical rule: an
    Owner-authored grant is still a grant, and 8.7.2 must bind it exactly as
    it binds any other, or the rule is not categorical. The corrected
    formulation confines pre-wall bootstrap to being outside 8.7's scope
    entirely (not an exemption from it, unchanged), moves every
    post-installation control-plane change to 8.7.6 as an Owner lifecycle
    action performed outside the grant system altogether (narrowly, to a
    control-plane-mutating recorder action and the named adoption-recorder
    action, leaving ordinary Part 7 closeout untouched), and gives the birth
    test itself the separate, exact, labeled falsification-probe schema of
    8.7.4.1-8.7.4.3 rather than a grant-based exemption: the label confers
    no authority and the runtime wall still denies the probe.

    D.4.7 Implement the protected-control-plane wall behavior directly under
    this work order. Rejected: WO-PL-023's own boundaries require Doctrine
    semantics to return to the Owner for ratification before any adapter
    implementation depends on them (Plan section 10); implementation and the
    required fresh birth test belong to WO-PL-024.

    D.4.8 Describe the deterministic dispatch-preparation tooling as
    optional convenience, leaving its necessity to distribution
    documentation alone. Rejected: Appendix B's own text instructs running
    "the project's pre-dispatch boundary generator" to produce the
    generated-boundaries block, so some such tooling is presupposed by the
    ratified workflow itself; describing it as optional was inconsistent
    with Doctrine's own appendix.

D.5 Boundaries of this decision:

    D.5.1 This ratifies Doctrine text only. It does not migrate Plumbline's
    own project-side governance instance off revision 0.6 (`STATE.md`:
    "Doctrine | Revision 0.6, ratified and effective"). DC.4.1 is unaffected:
    a separate Owner-ratified project-local decision and a
    `migration-guides/0.7-to-0.8.md` companion (WO-PL-023 B.3.3) are required
    before this repository's own governance instance binds to 0.8, exactly
    as 0.7 was ratified as a distribution package without migrating this
    repository's governance.

    D.5.2 This does not authorize any adapter, checker, template, skill, or
    migration-guide implementation. Materializing `DOCTRINE.md`,
    `decisions/DR-005.md`, and the repaired adopter contract is WO-PL-023
    phase 2, gated on this exact text being ratified without variance.
    Capability-wall hardening for Part 8.7 is WO-PL-024.

    D.5.3 This does not close RFI-22. RFI-22 remains OPEN pending the
    test-first adapter change and fresh provider birth test its own
    "Consequence of yes" section requires; this decision supplies the
    ratified normative basis that work will implement against.

    D.5.4 No enforcement claim is made by this decision for any provider,
    adapter, or repository. 8.7.2's rule becomes ratified text on
    ratification; 8.7.4 makes explicit that no wall is described as complying
    with it absent an observed birth test.
