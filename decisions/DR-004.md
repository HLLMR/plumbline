## Decision

Ratify the exact Doctrine 0.7 amendment described below. This record supplies
authority to materialize the ratified text under WO-PL-021. Until those exact
changes are present in `DOCTRINE.md`, the repository bytes continue to identify
0.6 as the current revision; once materialized, 0.7 is authoritative by this
decision. An Implementer may transcribe the decision but may not vary it.

DC.1 identifies revision 0.7 as Ratified, superseding 0.6, effective
2026-08-20 by this record. DC.2 gains a 0.7 row with this summary:

> Align Appendix B with the shipped pre-dispatch validator: classify every
> declared grant surface exactly once in `enforced_by` or
> `unenforced_boundaries`; default the provider-neutral template to no
> mechanically enforced whole surfaces; add generated-boundary markers and
> the checker-emission workflow. No other Doctrine clause changes.

Appendix B retains its eight declared minimum grant surfaces. Its default
classification is exactly:

```yaml
enforced_by: {}
unenforced_boundaries:
  - filesystem.write
  - filesystem.read.deny
  - shell.execute
  - network.egress
  - package.install
  - secrets.read
  - git.commit
  - git.push
```

Appendix B also contains exactly one
`<!-- BEGIN GENERATED BOUNDARIES -->` / `<!-- END GENERATED BOUNDARIES -->`
pair. The placeholder between them instructs the Owner to replace it with the
exact output of the project's pre-dispatch boundary generator after completing
frontmatter, and to validate the resulting work order before activation.

The standalone templates and self-contained adoption bundle are regenerated
from revision 0.7. A 0.6-to-0.7 migration guide requires an adopting project's
Owner to ratify migration, replace the local work-order template, generate the
boundaries block for each candidate, and validate before dispatch. Nothing
updates an adopting project automatically.

## Reasoning

The ratified 0.6 Appendix B and its shipped standalone copy declare eight grant
surfaces but classify only `filesystem.write`, and they omit the generated-
boundary markers required by the shipped validator. A new adopter can therefore
follow the canonical template and still be rejected by the canonical checker.
The work-order manifest is a Doctrine protocol boundary, so the correct repair
is to align the Doctrine, its extracted copies, its migration path, and its
public-process tests rather than document around the contradiction.

Defaulting every surface to unenforced is provider-neutral and fail-safe. An
Owner moves a surface into `enforced_by` only after naming and validating the
mechanism that covers the whole declared surface. The template therefore makes
no unearned enforcement claim.

## Rejected alternatives

1. Document the validator-only requirements without amending Appendix B.
   Rejected because the canonical template would remain structurally invalid.
2. Weaken the validator's complete-classification or generated-boundary rules.
   Rejected because both rules close observed dispatch-defect classes.
3. Keep `filesystem.write` mechanically enforced by default. Rejected because
   provider and mutation-channel coverage are project-specific facts.
4. Edit standalone or bundled templates without changing the Doctrine.
   Rejected because derived copies may not diverge from their appendix.
